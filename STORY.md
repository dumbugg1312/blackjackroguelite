# THE HOUSE — Narrative Bible v2 ("The Marker")

This document governs all story, dialogue, and copy. Voice: velvet, ominous, precise. Elegant dread, never campy. Second person, present tense. Short lines — nothing over ~20 words. Em-dashes and full stops, few exclamation points. The House speaks in ledgers, accounts, contracts, hospitality.

**v2 change of doctrine:** the story is now LINEAR, CHARACTER-BASED, and set in the REAL WORLD. Real proper nouns are not only allowed but required — the dread lives in the specificity. Every act of the descent advances one man's story, in order, with cause and effect.

## The world

**Biloxi, Mississippi. The night of October 3rd, 1957.**

At the end of Point Cadet, past the ice houses and the moored shrimp fleet, stands the **Magnolia Hotel**. Six stories of scorched brick and dead neon. It burned on the night of June 14th, 1927 — with a wedding still going on in the ballroom — and nobody rebuilt it, and nobody tore it down, and the fishermen have not looked directly at it in thirty years.

But ruined men find its doors. The Magnolia does not take money; Biloxi has a dozen joints on Highway 90 for money. The Magnolia plays for what you *are* — names, years, weddings, the color of a wife's eyes. Winners are paid in miracles. Losers walk home lighter. Below its lobby the hotel runs **twenty-one floors down**, which is twenty-one floors more than the county has on file, and the deepest floor holds everything it has ever won.

Its staff are its winnings too. Every dealer at every table is somebody the Magnolia kept.

## The characters

- **You — Harlan Creel, 44.** An oysterman's son. You ran oysters out of Biloxi harbor on a lugger named the *Miss Ada*, after your wife. You married Ada at St. Michael's in June of 1946; your father dealt cards at the reception, cleanly, for the only time in his life.
- **Ada Creel.** Your wife. When you became a stranger wearing her husband's clothes, she moved to her sister's in Mobile. She still writes. You keep the letters in a coffee tin, unanswered — you cannot hold her face in your mind long enough to write it a sentence.
- **Levi Creel — your father. Boss, Floor 14 ("the Cardsharp").** A card mechanic who dealt at the Magnolia to square his own debts. One winter night in 1943 he didn't come home. The House kept his hands, and the rest of him followed. He has been dealing in the Vaults ever since — and the House saves him for you deliberately, because it is cruel in exact proportion to what it knows.
- **Mr. Dorsey — Boss, Floor 7 ("the Bouncer").** The Magnolia's doorman since before the fire. Keeps the list of every guest the hotel ever admitted, and never lets one out early. Tonight he looked away and let you in — the House wanted you down; do not mistake the courtesy.
- **Elias Vann — the Concierge.** Bell captain of the Magnolia, 1931. The last man to beat the House at the final table. It rose, and offered him the chair, and he took it. What greets you at the Cage and pours in the Lounge is what twenty-six years of the chair have left of him. His arc: courtesy (Act I) → oblique warnings (Act II) → full confession and a plea (Act III).
- **Constance Perle — the Weeping Bride.** Married in the Magnolia ballroom on the night of June 14th, 1927. The fire took the wedding; the House took the bride. She sits at a dead table looking for her ring. (Shrine arc, 2 parts — unchanged mechanics.)
- **Abe Fisch — the Pawnbroker.** Keeps the only lit window on Point Cadet, across from the Magnolia's doors. The one living man in Biloxi who knows exactly what the hotel is, because he has been buying back what's left of its losers since 1931. Extends credit against your next attempt. Unimpressed by everything, including death.
- **THE HOUSE — final boss, Floor 21.** The thing that wears the Magnolia. It speaks as management.

## Your fall (the backstory, in order)

1. 1943 — your father Levi vanishes into the Magnolia. You are told never to walk down Point Cadet after dark.
2. June 1946 — you marry Ada at St. Michael's. Levi's empty chair at the reception is the only shadow on the day. *(The House will later claim it dealt that day. It lies the way ledgers lie — by entry.)*
3. 1949 — two bad seasons and a bank in New Orleans take the *Miss Ada*. You walk down Point Cadet in the rain, the way your father once did.
4. 1949–1951 — the Magnolia sits you down. Lends you a stake against yourself. You win, at first. Then, hand by hand: the boat's name, the June of your wedding, Ada's face, the color of her eyes.
5. 1951 — with nothing left, it deals one final hand. You sign the **Marker** — the deed to your soul, filed twenty-one floors down in a drawer with brass corners, in a vault beside the drawer marked CREEL, LEVI.
6. 1951–1957 — six hollow years. Food without taste. Sleep without dreams. Ada gone to Mobile. A town that has quietly stopped saying your name.
7. **Tonight** — October 3rd, 1957. The doors stand open. Mr. Dorsey looks away. You go down.

## Structure (linear, one descent)

- **Act I — The Parlors (floors 1–7):** the hotel's public rooms, preserved mid-1927: band music, dust on the champagne. The House is amused. It remembers your father; it condescends. Act ends at Mr. Dorsey's gate.
- **Act II — The Vaults (floors 8–14):** where the winnings are filed. Drawers hold named, specific things — the summer of 1946, Ada's face, CREEL, LEVI. The House turns interested, then uneasy. Act ends at your father's table.
- **Act III — The Pit (floors 15–21):** no music, no staff, only what the walls can send. Elias Vann confesses. The House is afraid, and begins offering to renegotiate. Ends at the last table, the Marker on the felt.

## Delivery mechanisms (all diegetic, all skippable — unchanged)

1. **Opening prologue** — three screens before floor 1: (1) the Magnolia and Biloxi, (2) Harlan's fall, (3) tonight and the stakes.
2. **Act interstitials** — floors 8 and 15, each opening with a progress marker (dealers beaten, floors left).
3. **The Voice of the House** — one whisper per landing, act-pooled, escalating courtesy → curiosity → fear. Now addressed to *Mr. Creel* by name, with real details (the boat, Ada's letters, Levi's hands).
4. **Dealer dialogue** — per-enemy registers unchanged; the dealers are hotel staff the House kept.
5. **The Concierge (Elias Vann)** — shop/parlor/lounge greetings by act, three scripted shrine encounters, Act III confession.
6. **Relic/charm flavor** — italic in-world tooltip lines.
7. **Death epitaphs** — keyed to cause, plus the standing line "THE HOUSE KEEPS YOUR BLOOD ON ACCOUNT."

## The Concierge's confession (Act III, verbatim)

> "My name was Elias Vann. Bell captain, 1931. The last man to beat this House was me."
> "I won my Marker at the last table. Then the House rose, and offered me the chair."
> "Twenty-six years I have carried its keys. When it offers you the chair — and it will — choose the door. I didn't."

## Endings (after The House falls — the candle, the chair, the Marker on the felt)

- **Take the Marker (Escape):** "You burn it in the candle. Six years go up like fog off the Sound. Upstairs, the doors stand open on a gray Gulf morning — and for the first time since 1951, you remember the color of her eyes. There is a letter in your pocket you finally know how to answer. — THE DEBT IS PAID."
- **Take the Chair (Inheritance):** "The House rises to let you sit. It always needed a dealer more than a debtor. Somewhere above, the doors of the Magnolia forget to lock — and out on Point Cadet, a ruined man turns his collar to the rain." — the title screen subtitle changes for this profile (under new management).

## Copy rules

Real proper nouns are required where they ground the story (Biloxi, Point Cadet, Mobile, St. Michael's, Highway 90, the *Miss Ada*, 1927, 1957). No modern idiom ("OK", "cool"), no fourth-wall UI talk inside dialogue, no lore dumps over five lines, no jump-scare all-caps outside authored banners. Named characters stay consistent everywhere: Harlan Creel, Ada, Levi Creel, Mr. Dorsey, Elias Vann, Constance Perle, Abe Fisch.
