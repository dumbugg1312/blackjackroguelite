# THE HOUSE — Narrative Bible ("The Second Deal")

This document governs all story, dialogue, and copy. PRODUCT.md voice rules still apply: velvet, ominous, precise. Elegant dread, never campy. Second person, present tense. Short lines — nothing over ~18 words. Em-dashes and full stops, few exclamation points. The House speaks in ledgers, accounts, contracts, hospitality.

## Premise

Years ago you sat down at the House's table and lost everything a person can sign away: your name, your years, the color of your eyes. It's all held against your **Marker** — your soul as collateral, filed twenty-one floors down. Tonight the doors forgot to lock. You are **the Debtor**, and you have come to win it back or be filed forever.

## Structure

- **Act I — The Parlors (floors 1–7):** The House is amused. Dealers are polite. It underestimates you.
- **Act II — The Vaults (floors 8–14):** The House is interested. It shows you what it keeps. It starts making offers.
- **Act III — The Pit (floors 15–21):** The House is afraid. No more music. It sends family.

## Delivery mechanisms (all diegetic, all skippable)

1. **Opening vignette** (one screen after New Descent, before floor 1):
   > You signed the Marker in a warmer decade. Your name, your years, the color of your eyes — the House keeps them all on account.
   > Tonight the doors forgot to lock.
   > Twenty-one floors down, the House is still dealing.
   > Go and be dealt in.

2. **Act interstitials** (full-screen beats at floors 8 and 15):
   - Act II: "The Vaults. Here the House keeps what it won: names, weddings, summers. Somewhere below, in a drawer with brass corners — yours. It knows you're coming. The cards are getting heavier."
   - Act III: "The Pit. No music down here. Just the sound the shoe makes when it's afraid. The House has stopped sending dealers. Now it sends family."

3. **The Voice of the House** — one whispered line on each landing (door-choice) screen, drawn from act-specific pools (≥8 lines per act, escalating from courtesy → curiosity → fear).

4. **Dealer dialogue** — every enemy gets: 1 intro line, 2–3 mid-fight barks (triggered on: player blackjack, dealer win, dealer at <30% HP), 1 death line. Barks appear as a speech line near the portrait, never blocking play. Write them in each dealer's own register (the Usher is kind; the Weeper apologizes; the Auditor cites policy; Velvet Widow flirts like a trap).

5. **The Concierge** — recurring NPC. He was the last debtor to beat the House — and he took the chair; what serves you at the Cage and haunts certain shrines is what's left of him. Appears: shop greetings (act-specific), the Parlor, and three scripted encounters (see Shrine arcs). Arc: courtesy (Act I) → oblique warnings (Act II) → confession and a plea (Act III): *"When it offers you the chair — and it will — choose the door. I didn't."* His lines never exceed two sentences at a time.

6. **Relic/charm flavor** — every relic and charm gains one italic flavor line in its tooltip, in-world ("Cold Coffee — *the night auditor never finished it.*").

7. **Death screen** — rotate ≥6 narrative epitaphs keyed to how you died (bust, dealer win, boss, debt) plus the standing line "THE HOUSE KEEPS YOUR BLOOD ON ACCOUNT."

## Boss exchanges (authored, use verbatim; extend in same voice as needed)

- **The Bouncer (7):** intro: "You're not on the list. You're in it." · phase 2: "The list is getting shorter." · death: "He checks his ledger one last time — and files you under *pending*."
- **The Cardsharp (14):** intro: "I dealt your wedding, you know. Lovely hand. Shame how it played out." · on swap: "Let me fix that for you." · death: "He fans himself out on the felt — every card a face you almost remember."
- **THE HOUSE (21):** intro: "Sit. You've come twenty floors to hear me say it: the Marker is real. Your signature is real. Nothing in this room is yours — including the chair." · phase 2 banner: "THE FINE PRINT." · at low HP: "We can renegotiate."

## Shrine arcs (chained events, tracked per run)

- **The Weeping Bride (2 parts):** Part 1 (Act I–II shrine): a bride at a dead table asks you to find her ring. Part 2 (later shrine): return the Gilded ring card you've been carrying (it entered your shoe in Part 1) for a real reward, or keep it. Choices remember.
- **The Concierge encounters (3 scripted):** Act I: he refuses your tip. Act II: he shows you a drawer with your name on it — one free peek at a relic choice. Act III: the confession (above).

## Endings (the final choice — after The House falls)

A single choice screen. The candle. The chair. The Marker on the felt.

- **Take the Marker (Escape):** "You burn it in the candle. It goes up like a decade. Upstairs, the doors stand open on a gray morning you have never seen before. — THE DEBT IS PAID."
- **Take the Chair (Inheritance):** "The House rises to let you sit. It always needed a dealer more than a debtor. Somewhere upstairs, the doors forget to lock." — the title screen afterward changes subtly for this profile (the subtitle line acknowledges the new management).

Both endings tally separately in lifetime stats (Escapes / Inheritances) and on the victory screen.

## Copy bans

No modern idiom ("OK", "cool", "let's go"), no fourth-wall UI talk inside dialogue, no lore dumps over four lines, no proper nouns from the real world, no jump-scare all-caps outside authored banners.
